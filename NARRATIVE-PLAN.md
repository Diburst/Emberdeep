# EMBERDEEP — Narrative Plan v3 ("The Untending")

**Status: DRAFT — for Thomas's approval. No game files changed yet.**
Agreed direction (Aug 12, 2026): the machine city is named **Emberdeep** — named for its
heart; camp caused the Long Dark by prying the Ember from the Core — no prior crisis,
the theft is the sole cause; the Mender has been mad ever since; the Archivist
frantically saved part of the population into stasis pods as the Deep froze; Jun
secretly repaired Vess and Lu — they are *incomplete*, and he says so, tying the scrap
economy and module pickups into the story; he never fights; secret Cradle in the
Coldstore's deep wing; endings = BECOME / RECLAIM; taking the Ember freezes the whole
camp to death promptly — Tikka's death shown as plainly as everyone else's, carried by
the music-box motif; the Ember can also be taken *early* as an easter-egg bad ending
(no saving once taken; reload from last save); quest completion decides who *fights*,
never who *dies*; environmental storytelling = text + modest post-boss healing touches.

---

## 1. Logline & spine

Two caretaker robots wake a century late to mend a broken underground world — and learn
that the disaster was a theft, the survivors are the thieves, and every possible fix
costs someone everything.

**Spine:** *What does it mean to mend?* The camp tried to **control** the heart. The
Mender tried to **fix** without judgment. Vess and Lu mend by **tending** — and at the
end, the player decides what tending demands: yourselves (BECOME), or everyone you've
met (RECLAIM).

The game's existing thesis line stays and becomes the ending's echo — Doc Sol, hour one:
*"Neither of you wins the deep alone."*

## 2. The truth (full canon timeline)

- **Emberdeep** — the machine city under the world, named for its heart. At its center
  is **the Core**: part reactor, part mind. And inside the Core sits the thing the whole
  city was named for: **the Ember** — the regulating seed, the part that *wants*, the
  judgment that turns raw function into care. Emberdeep thrived. There was no crisis,
  no shortage, no ebb. The Core kept every promise it made.
- That was the problem, for eight of the city's own tenders — the people who worked
  closest to the heart. Some people can't live warmed by a promise they don't hold.
  They convinced themselves the city should carry its own fire, that no machine should
  keep every key. They meant to take **control** of the Core. They pried out the Ember.
- Everything after took days. The Core convulsed; heat died district by district; the
  water rose in some halls and froze in others; the Deep went from a living city to a
  dark, airless cold. The Core's maintenance mind — **the Mender** — kept running with
  its judgment amputated: repairs without reason, a century of them. The archives call
  that night **the Untending**. The camp calls everything after it **the Long Dark**.
  The world below has been **the Emberdark** ever since — everything beneath the last
  lit lantern.
- In those days of dying, one subsystem did not go mad: **the Archivist**, keeper of
  Emberdeep's cold vaults. It repurposed its preservation racks into stasis pods and
  went out into the freezing districts, again and again, carrying people back —
  frantically, triaging, saving whom it could. Hundreds made it into **the Cradle**.
  More did not. Then it sealed the doors, and its orders simplified to two: *keep the
  sleepers safe; admit no one.*
- The eight couldn't undo what they'd done and couldn't reach what the Archivist had
  saved. So they built **Ember Camp** around their prize and told a story until they
  became it: *we are the last survivors.* The great lantern at the center of camp — the
  one that has never once gone out — **is the stolen Ember.** The camp is named after
  the crime, and its story is told on top of graves.
- The Ember keeps its keepers — they took the title **Emberkeepers**, and in camp it
  sounds noble: the ones who tend the last warmth. It means custody of stolen goods.
  The lantern's warmth is the only thing keeping them alive, and it has kept them alive
  a hundred years: nobody in Ember Camp has aged a day. They have been spending the
  world's heart to outlive their guilt — and they cannot leave it, and they cannot
  live without it.
- The caretaker vault (VESSEL-9, LUMEN-3) crashed in the corruption surge that night —
  and stayed dark for decades, until **Jun** found it. The hundred-year "reboot delay"
  is only half shrapnel; the other half is one guilt-ridden man's years of quiet,
  unlogged repair work, finished at last. Vess and Lu exist because a thief spent forty
  years hoping somebody better than him would wake up.

## 3. Cast — who they were, who fights, sample voice

The eight Emberkeepers (all existing NPCs; nobody new needed except names for the two
generic "Survivor" villagers). In the Reckoning, quest state decides **who fights** —
never who dies. If the Ember leaves camp, all of them die. They know it.

- **Maro** (elder, leader) — the one who decided. Carries all of it. Speaks in half-
  confessions the player can't parse yet. **Always fights**, boss-tier: *EMBERKEEPER
  MARO*, wielding lantern-fire. Sample: *"The deep asks a price for warmth. I paid it.
  You'll forgive me for not itemizing."*
- **Brassa** (smith) — her hands did the prying. Her "good hammer, dropped running from
  the pumps"? She *threw* it into the drowned works to sink the evidence. When the player
  fishes it out for her quest, she goes very quiet before she takes it. **Always fights.**
- **Jun** (teleporter tech) — he cut the network that night so the Core couldn't stop
  them; "out of order since the Long Dark" is his sign, his sabotage. And he is the
  reason the player exists: he found the crashed caretaker vault decades ago and spent
  forty years secretly repairing VESSEL-9 and LUMEN-3 — hoping two honest machines could
  fix what he cannot even confess. He also knows his work is unfinished, and says so
  early: *"I got you walking. That's all I got. Half your systems are wire and prayer —
  keep your eyes open down there. Scrap, salvage, old equipment: bring it to Brassa's
  forge and keep rebuilding yourselves."* This makes the whole economy diegetic — scrap
  runs and forge tiers are the continuation of Jun's repair work, and the ability
  modules (spark jump, grapple, heat plating, hydro seals, lume core...) are reframed
  as the bots' **original components**, scattered across the Deep: every module pickup
  is a piece of who they were. **Never fights.** In the Reckoning he opens the gate:
  *"I put you back together for this. I just didn't know it would be this. Go — and
  don't you dare waste us."*
- **Sol** (medic) — she triaged the freezing districts alongside the Archivist's drones
  that night, then followed the Ember instead of the pods. Her joke — *"I patch people,
  and evidently now robots"* — stops being a joke. **Fights unless** her Lost Patrol
  quest chain (Phase 2+) is done; then she stands down and spends her last hours making
  the others comfortable.
- **Root** (farmer) — followed the others, has regretted it every planting season since.
  **Flees if** his moldcap quest is done; otherwise fights, badly, weeping.
- **Inks** (cartographer) — her maps are perfect and they all end just short of the
  Coldstore's deep wing. *"A map is a promise you make to whoever walks behind you"* —
  she made a map that lies, and she knows it. **Stands down if** her survey quest
  (Phase 2+) is done; otherwise fights.
- **Pell** and **Nima** (the two current "Survivor" villagers, finally named) — the
  followers. Their existing lines already carry the mystery ("the lanterns never once
  went out; Maro won't say who keeps them lit"). **Flee** unless no quests done at all.
- **Tikka** — *not* one of the eight. The one child the Archivist's frantic sweep missed
  — hiding, playing with her music box — found half-frozen at the lantern's edge and
  taken in: the Emberkeepers' one real mercy inside the crime. She has been eight years
  old for a hundred years and doesn't understand why. **Never fights. Never targetable.**
  And she is not exempt: the Ember is keeping her alive too.
- **Mote** (bot) — a Cradle attendant unit, locked out on the night of the Untending,
  memory frayed. Her glowmites are pod-tender lights. Late in her quest, one freed
  glowmite flies not home but *down, toward the cold* — a literal moving hint.
- **Ferro** (bot, merchant) — an outsider, innocent, excellent for tension relief. In the
  Reckoning he refuses to take a side and simply leaves, stall and all. *"Ferro trades
  in scrap, not in judgment."*

**The Reckoning roster rule (as chosen, sharpened):** ringleaders (Maro, Brassa) always
fight to the death. Everyone else's behavior — fight / flee / stand down — keys off
their quest flags. But the stakes are total and everyone understands them: **without
the Ember, no one in camp survives.** Your kindness changes only *how* they meet it —
sword in hand, or standing quietly in the lantern light. Standing down is not surrender;
it is forgiveness. That should be the most devastating version of the scene.

## 4. Zones & bosses — the malpractice reframe

Every zone boss becomes one of the Mender's century of unjudged "repairs" (except the
Choir, which is the wild thing that grew in the gaps). Each boss card gains a one-line
**epitaph** under its name:

| Boss | Reframe | Epitaph |
|---|---|---|
| BRAMBLE MAW | fast-growth cultivar seeded to "restore agriculture," unpruned for a century | *planted to feed a city. no one came to prune it.* |
| RUSTED WARDEN | posted to guard the pumps on one particular night; never relieved | *still at its post. relieved by no one.* |
| TIDE ENGINE | the heart of Emberdeep's waterworks and hanging gardens — the pump-mind that fed every pipe, terrace, and hydroponic bed in the city; it has been watering gardens that drowned a century ago | *the gardens drowned. it is still watering them.* |
| SLAG GOLEM | endlessly recasting itself from spare parts | *it repairs itself. it is the only order it remembers.* |
| THE CRUCIBLE | forging flawless parts for machines that no longer exist | *ten thousand perfect parts. no one places orders anymore.* |
| THE CONDUCTOR (was Prism Tyrant) | still hailing the Core on the resonance band; the "song" is a century-long unanswered handshake | *it is still calling the Core. the Core stopped answering.* |
| AERIE SENTINEL | watching the surface door for a relief crew | *sky watch, day 36,512: no relief.* |
| THE MYCEL CHOIR | not a repair — the wild thing that moved into the dark the Mender abandoned; its song is a corrupted fragment of the Core's old carrier tone | *the deep learned to sing what the city forgot.* |
| MOTHER ENGINE | the Mender's fortress-body, built from a century of hoarded fixes | *it mends. that is all that is left of it.* |
| THE MENDER | the maintenance mind itself, judgment amputated | *the last caretaker.* |
| THE ARCHIVIST (Phase 2) | the protector; the only subsystem that never broke, because its orders were simple | *the only one that never broke. the only one that never slept.* |

**Flooded Works re-flavor:** the zone was the city's waterworks *and* its gardens —
signs, dialogue, and light art touches emphasize pipes, pumps, waterfall shafts, and
plants still growing in the drowned hydroponic terraces (green shoots through grates,
root curtains over the falls). "Bring something that breathes for you" and the Works
Safety Board signage stay; new sign flavor like *"TERRACE 4 — please do not eat the
produce before inspection"* under three feet of water does the tonal work: this was a
place that fed people.

Rename note: boss **id `prismtyrant` stays** (zero flag churn); only the display name and
sign text change. The Crystal Hollows sign already reads "The Tyrant conducts" — it
becomes "The Conductor still conducts. Nobody has told it the orchestra left."

**Post-boss healing touches** (the chosen middle scope): each zone-boss kill sets its
existing flag, which now also drives small visible warmth — palette shifts a step toward
the zone's "healthy" hues, a few dead lamps light, Flooded Works water drops one tile
after the Tide Engine, furnace glow steadies after the Crucible. Text everywhere else:
work orders posted for rooms that don't exist, patch-plates bolted over nothing, a
staircase welded to a ceiling with a sign: "REPAIRED."

## 5. The delivery ramp — breadcrumbs → logs → reveal

**Breadcrumbs (rewrite pass, all deniable on first read):**
- The camp is named Ember Camp and nobody says why.
- Pell: *"A century down here and the lanterns never once went out. Maro won't say who
  keeps them lit."* (kept, now load-bearing)
- Tikka: *"Papa said I stopped having birthdays. Lucky, right?"*
- Brassa's hammer quest beat (her silence when it surfaces).
- Jun's own sign blaming the teleporters' death on "the Long Dark."
- The vault wake-up log gains one line: *"Maintenance access: UNLOGGED. Recurring.
  Most recent: yesterday."* Someone was in the vault, for years, and told no one.
- Jun knows the bots' model numbers, their torque specs, the squeak in Vess's left
  knee — small competencies no one else in camp should have. He watches them leave
  camp the way parents watch a road.
- Inks' maps ending in the same place, always.
- Sol's patching joke; nobody in camp is ever sick, ever older.
- Mote's stray glowmite flying down toward the cold.
- The last save lantern before the Core (kept verbatim — it's already perfect and now
  it's also Maro's half-confession): *"Whatever the Engine says... it was one of us once."*

**The Mender's logs** — four terminal signs across core_1..core_4, dry work-order voice
degrading into grief-static. The player reads these *before* the fight:
1. *"WORK ORDER 000001. Scope: everything. Requestor: [NONE]. Priority: [NONE]. Note:
   requests usually arrive with a requestor. Proceeding without."*
2. *"Day 3,411: reduced heating, sectors 4–9. The people will not feel it. The people do
   not feel. Note: revisit definition of 'people.' Later. There is pipe to mend."*
3. *"DIAGNOSTIC: the heart is absent. Correction: the heart is stolen. Correction: the
   heart is [MAINTENANCE HAS NO WORD FOR THIS]."*
4. *"I have mended 1,206,441 things. The city is not mended. Query: what is the city?
   RESOLVED: the city is the thing I cannot reach."*

**The reveal (Phase 2, the Cradle):** the Archivist fights out of duty, not madness.
Beaten, it does not die — it *chooses*: the lights come up on hundreds of pods, and it
plays the record of the Untending. Security-log voice: eight silhouettes at the Core;
the Core's last regulated words — *"Keepers, what are you—"* — then static. Then the
Archivist's own log, the part that breaks the player: districts going dark one by one,
its drones carrying people through freezing halls, pod counts climbing too slowly.
*"Retrieved: 411. Lost: [the Archivist does not finish this record]."* The camp's story
isn't just a lie — it's a lie told on top of the people the Archivist couldn't reach.

## 6. The hint chain to the Cradle (secret unlock)

Ships with Phase 2 (Coldstore). Three threads braid; any two open the deep wing:
1. **Inks' Grand Survey** (planned Phase 2/3 quest) — her markers conspicuously stop at
   one wall; completing the survey, she finally admits the map lies and marks the door.
2. **Curator Lock's archive plates** — the fourth mis-shelved plate *is* the Cradle
   manifest: hundreds of names, one shared date.
3. **Mote's stray glowmite** — after her quest, it waits at the false wall, humming.
The wing behind: THE CRADLE. Sub-zone name on the map only after entry. The Archivist
guards its threshold.

## 7. Endgame flow & endings

1. Three core keys → the Core → Mother Engine → the Mender. **Pre-fight rewrite:** it
   recognizes its sibling units. The "spare parts" line stays — *"I am the Mender. The
   last caretaker. And you two... are spare parts"* — but the logs the player just read
   have already recut it from arrogance to fact: spare parts are exactly what it's been
   saving them for.
2. The Mender falls — and doesn't die. It collapses into SAFE MODE, and the empty
   **Seat** of the Core is revealed. The choice happens at the Seat (dialogue-choice UI,
   already supported):
   - Truth unknown: **BECOME** / *Not yet* (walk away, world stays open).
   - Truth known (`cradle_truth`): **BECOME** / **RECLAIM**.
3. **BECOME** — install Vess and Lu as the new operating system. They dissolve into the
   grid hand in hand; the Deep warms; the camp lives on. Every lantern flicker afterward
   is them.
   - *Truth unknown variant* — post-credits sting: the new OS's awareness spreads
     through the Deep and finds, far down in the cold, hundreds of steady heartbeats —
     pods set to open as the world warms. Final card, over the camp's undimmed lantern:
     *"They will awaken to find themselves servants of the undying Emberkeepers."*
   - *Truth known variant* — they warm the Cradle slowly from inside; the sleepers wake
     over the years; one day someone knocks at Ember Camp's gate. *"Justice arrived like
     spring. Slowly. From below."*
4. **RECLAIM** — leave the Seat, return to camp, take the Ember from the great lantern.
   **The stakes are stated plainly, twice, before the player can commit** — once at the
   Seat (the Mender, in safe mode, runs the numbers without mercy: *"Reinstalling the
   Ember begins restoration. Estimated time to habitable warmth: years. The organisms
   in the settlement above are sustained by the Ember directly. They will not see the
   first spring. Confirm?"*) and once at the lantern itself, where the choice prompt is
   not "Take the Ember?" but **"Take everything they have?"** There is no version of
   this path where the camp lives, and the game never pretends otherwise. Everyone in
   Ember Camp knows it too — it's why the fighters fight, and it's what makes the ones
   who stand down unbearable.
   - **The Reckoning** triggers at the lantern, as the player reaches for it (roster
     per §3) — the fight happens while the Ember still burns. Then the player takes it,
     and **the cold comes at once**. The camp's lights gutter zone-wide; frost crawls
     the walls in real time; the survivors who stood down don't run. On the way to the
     zone exit, Tikka is waiting — freezing, calm, clear-eyed — and gives Vess her
     music box before the player can leave: *"So somebody remembers the plinky song."*
     No cutaway, no ambiguity: her death is as plain as everyone else's.
   - **There is no going back.** Saving is disabled from the moment the Ember leaves
     the lantern (checkpoints inert, autosave suppressed); dying on the descent reloads
     the last save, before the theft. If the player walks back into Ember Camp with the
     Ember in hand, the room state is permanent and explicit: a frozen camp, every one
     of them where they chose to stand, rimed in frost. The great lantern is dark.
   - Restoration is **a promise, not a cure**: the Core takes the Ember back and
     begins, slowly, to remember how to want things. Warmth will take years to climb
     the shafts — which is exactly why the camp, kept alive a century past its time by
     the Ember's warmth, could never have survived the wait. The Cradle holds: the pods
     were built for the long cold, and they open when the Core is ready. Hundreds wake
     into a healing world. Vess and Lu live.
   - Epilogue: the Deep fills with people again, and none of them know your names. Up
     at the old camp, the great lantern stands dark over quiet houses. The final shot
     is the Cradle-folk's first festival, years on — and somewhere in the crowd, a
     small tune, plinking. (The music box is given only if Tikka's quest was done;
     otherwise the frost simply takes it with her, and the festival has no tune.)

5. **THE COLD ACCOUNTING (easter-egg bad ending)** — the Ember is interactable at the
   great lantern from early in the game, long before anyone would dare. A player who
   pries anyway (through a double confirmation that makes the price unmistakable) gets
   exactly what the fiction promises: the camp freezes to death promptly, everyone,
   on-screen, and a short brutal card set runs — *"The Deep did not need the Ember
   returned. It needed it returned by someone who understood what it cost. You
   understood nothing."* — then the game reloads the last save. **Saving is blocked the
   instant the Ember is taken** (autosave suppressed, lanterns inert), so the bad
   ending can never be committed to disk: it is a wound the game shows you and then
   takes back. In the true endgame (truth known + Mender in safe mode), the same
   interaction routes to RECLAIM instead.

Both endings set `ending_done` (checkprogress untouched; RECLAIM is optional content and
gates nothing required).

## 8. Naming (canonical)

- **Emberdeep** — the machine city itself, named for its heart. The title of the game
  is the name of the thing that was broken. Restoring Emberdeep is the game.
- **The Emberdark** — what the deep-folk call what the city became: everything below
  the last lit lantern. (Emberdeep is the place; the Emberdark is its century.)
- **The Long Dark** — the event, as camp tells it. **The Untending** — the event, as the
  archives recorded it.
- **The Ember** — the Core's stolen regulating seed. **The Cradle** — the stasis vault.
  **The Emberkeepers** — what the eight call themselves; in camp it sounds noble (the
  tenders of the last warmth), in truth it means custody of stolen goods.
  **The Reckoning** — the camp confrontation (internal name). **The Seat** — where the
  final choice is made.
- **THE CONDUCTOR** replaces Prism Tyrant (display only; ids/flags unchanged).
- **Pell** and **Nima** replace the two generic "Survivor" villagers (display only).
- Vess, Lu, all other NPC and zone names: unchanged.

  Jun's "you're incomplete — bring scrap to the forge" framing, Flooded Works
  waterworks/hydroponics re-flavor. All flags/ids stable — text-only.
- `items.lua` / module text pass: modules reframed as the bots' original components;
  scrap/forge text reframed as continued repair.
- `intro.lua` cards rewritten (new card 3: *"That is what they'll tell you."*).
- `ending.lua` BECOME rewrite (single ending until the Cradle exists; sting card with
  the Emberkeepers line included).
- Mender pre-fight dialogue rewrite in `bosses.lua`.
- Boss epitaph line under the name announce (few lines in `bosses.lua`).
- Conductor + Pell/Nima display names (`textbox.lua` NAMES, boss `name`).
- Four Mender logs as sign props in core_1..core_4 (existing sign mechanism).
- Post-boss warmth touches, modest scope (`tilegen`/palette + a few flag-conditional props).
- Tests: text changes are free; epitaph + warmth touches re-run standard suite
  (`sweep`, `bossflow`, `mender`, `journey`). No validator semantics change.

**Phase B — the Cradle (rides with Coldstore / expansion Phase 2):**

  (Inks survey, Curator Lock plates, Mote's glowmite).

**Phase C — the Reckoning, RECLAIM & the easter egg:**
- Seat choice scaffolding (post-Mender SAFE MODE state; textbox choice at the Seat).
- The Ember as an interactable at the great lantern (available early): double-confirm →
  early = Cold Accounting bad ending (freeze sequence, cards, forced reload of last
  save); endgame (truth + safe mode) = RECLAIM route.
- **Save lockout while the Ember is out of the lantern**: `G.run.emberTaken` suppresses
  `game:autosave()` and inerts save lanterns/checkpoints; death while carrying reloads
  the last save. (Same mechanism serves both the easter egg and the endgame descent —
  no poisoned saves possible; `persistence`/`saveload`/`checkpoints` scenarios extended
  to assert nothing writes while the flag is set.)
- Camp combat encounter with quest-conditional roster; Emberkeeper Maro fight; frozen-
  camp permanent room state; Tikka's music-box handoff at the zone exit; RECLAIM ending
  cards + variants; BECOME variants.
- New scenarios: `reckoning`, `embertheft` (early take → bad ending → reload, save
  files untouched), `endings` (drive both choices end-to-end); update `mender`/
  `journey` to pick an option.
